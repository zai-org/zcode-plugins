# Archetypal Figures in Dreams

Starting points for Phase 4 (archetypal mapping) of the analysis. These are *roles the psyche casts*, not fixed characters. Offer each as a hypothesis and let the dreamer verify it against their associations. An archetype shows itself by its **numinosity** — the figure feels other, larger, or more charged than ordinary dream people.

For each figure: how it typically appears, its dynamic, and questions that test the fit.

## Persona
- **Appears as**: uniforms, official roles, masks, the "presentable" self, crowds of identical people, being unable to remove makeup or a costume.
- **Dynamic**: the compromise between individual and society. Problems arise when identified with it (rigid, hollow role-life) or without it (no functioning place in the collective).
- **Ask**: "In waking life, where are you playing exactly this role? Is it a coat you wear — or a skin?"

## Shadow
- **Appears as**: the pursuer, the threatening stranger, the despised or criminal figure, same-sex characters the dreamer fears or scorns; often dark, dirty, of the "wrong" people-group in a personal sense.
- **Dynamic**: the disowned, repressed or undeveloped part of the personality — usually with real vitality. Chased by it: avoiding one's own rejected qualities. Fighting it: moral conflict with one's own darkness. Integrating it (it turns helpful): a major step in development.
- **Ask**: "What quality of this figure, if you had it, would be dangerous or embarrassing? And is there anything in it that is actually alive and needed?"

## Anima / Animus
- **Appears as**: a significant contra-sexual figure — for a man, a woman (muse, witch, lover, lost bride, stranger who guides); for a woman, a man (faceless lover, concertizing figure, unknown guide, often appearing in a series of men).
- **Dynamic**: the soul-image, the bridge to the inner world and to relatedness. In a series, watch how this figure develops — it often carries the individuation movement itself.
- **Ask**: "Who is this figure to you? Does anyone in your life carry this face — and what part of your own inner life might they be standing in for?"

## The Self
- **Appears as**: circles, mandalas, quaternities, the sacred or royal couple, a wise or divine child, a treasure hard to attain, the far center of the journey, a jewel, an egg, or an overwhelming figure of unity (Christ, Buddha, the world-tree).
- **Dynamic**: the totality of the psyche, the ordering center beyond the ego. Its symbols often arrive at decisive turns in life; encounters feel numinous, frightening or blissful.
- **Ask**: "Where in the dream is the true center — what would everything in it point to if it were a story?"

## Great Mother
- **Appears as**: the nurturing mother, but also the devouring or terrible mother — a house that swallows, a witch, a sea-monster, an engulfing flood, a mother whose embrace smothers; also as fate, nature, the earth, the womb-like vessel.
- **Dynamic**: the archetypal mother behind one's personal mother — nourishment, protection, but also engulfment, refusal to let go, regression.
- **Ask**: "Is this figure feeding you or consuming you? Where does your own life have this same shape?"

## Wise Old Man / Wise Woman
- **Appears as**: teacher, doctor, priest, grandfather/grandmother, a helpful stranger with knowledge, sometimes a talking animal or a ghost.
- **Dynamic**: the spirit of knowledge and meaning — often appears when the dreamer's conscious understanding has run out. Its shadow: the guru who enthralls, or rationalization that explains everything away.
- **Ask**: "What does this figure tell you — and do you *know* this already, somewhere in yourself?"

## Divine Child
- **Appears as**: a baby or child that is threatened, abandoned, or miraculous; the small hero; something precious and vulnerable carried through the dream.
- **Dynamic**: the new potential becoming conscious — a future personality, an idea at its beginning. Often surrounded by danger because new life is fragile.
- **Ask**: "What is young and new in your life that this dream is trying to protect — or asking you to take seriously?"

## Trickster
- **Appears as**: clown, fool, animal (coyote, raven, fox), a comic figure who breaks rules, absurd reversals of the plot.
- **Dynamic**: primitive, undifferentiated energy; also the reliever of rigidity — the dream laughing at the ego's seriousness.
- **Ask**: "Where did the dream break your expectations? What were you taking too solemnly?"

## Hero
- **Appears as**: the dreamer or a young figure facing a monster, a task, a dragon, a fight, a rescue; weapons, quests, contests.
- **Dynamic**: the ego's struggle for autonomy and consciousness against the archetypal — necessary but dangerous when it stays stuck in conquest.
- **Ask**: "What is being fought here — and what would winning this fight actually win for you?"

## Note on the ego in the dream

Always note **who the dreamer is in the dream** — observer, actor, victim, absent. The ego's position in the dream usually mirrors the ego's position in the psychic drama of waking life: watching from the balcony, being carried, standing helpless, acting decisively.
