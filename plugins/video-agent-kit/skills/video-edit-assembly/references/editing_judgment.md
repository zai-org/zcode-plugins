# Editing Judgment for Multi-Material Assembly

Select, structure and review for readable subjects and worthwhile moments. Adapt pace to the viewer. Implementation belongs in [timeline.md](timeline.md); keep decisions in the project's existing notes.

## Read the Brief as Viewer Value

Map important phrases to visible or audible evidence:

- "Interaction" needs an exchange or relationship the viewer can perceive.
- "Highlight" or "focus on" gives the subject readable scale, time and emphasis.
- "Complete process" includes setup, action and result when the source contains them.
- Mood words such as "warm", "fun" or "calm" guide the chosen moments, pacing and ending.
- "All content" may mean events, files or every source second. Under a tight, ambiguous brief, preserve the main events and disclose omissions; use extreme speed changes only when source-time coverage is the priority.
- "Natural photo insertion" places photos where they support an action, memory or pause.

Honor explicit scene, sequence and duration requirements. If observed material cannot support a named scene or relationship, disclose the gap; a substitute must remain faithful to what the footage shows.

## Select for New Information

Group ranges by activity across files. Locate each section's defining action, reaction or discovery and record its source interval, including necessary setup and aftermath. Choose support for what it adds in context: an exchange, expression, clearer detail, useful sound or required coverage. A describable movement in every shot is not necessarily a new reason to keep watching; distinguish a meaningful development from another example of the same behavior.

Read the whole selection as activity totals, including later returns, to locate cumulative repetition. Focus first on the greatest concern; comparing one activity does not settle other long repeated passages. If the brief permits alternatives and repetition is genuinely in question, use the [comparison method](selection_comparison.md) to inspect current and tighter rough cuts. Prefer a shorter cut only when it preserves meaningful development and the required content and reference pacing.

Continuity can be the attraction in a performance, conversation, sports attempt or quiet observation. Identify the behavior, sound, light or spatial development that needs time. A performance's phrases and interactions matter more than representing each source file. Keep separate attempts distinguishable; never invent a success or causal exchange from fragments. Unheard sound remains unverified, not proof of a passage's quality.

## Allocate Time by Information Density

Simple views can register quickly; faces, signs, group photos and interactions need reading time. Vary duration with content; without a requested duration, let worthwhile moments determine length.

For photos, consider complexity as well as duration: a collage may need fewer images or more space. Insert a face, detail or pause where it contributes; keep an unresolved action together. A near-identical photo after a video needs a reason to repeat the view. A clear still is valid. For purposeful movement, use [photo_motion.md](photo_motion.md) and its temporal checks; sparse stills cannot reveal high-frequency shake.

Faster playback can condense travel or setup if comprehension survives. Slow motion is useful only when the important event is already readable; fix weak selection or framing before extending it.

## Build a Structure

State what the viewer should remember, supported by actual footage. Give that attraction more weight than routine coverage. Judge supporting scenes by their context, contrast or breathing room and by how long they delay the next worthwhile moment.

Inspect viable opening ranges and record why the choice introduces the subject and invites attention. Assess the whole lead-in: attractive cards and establishing views can still delay the first meaningful moment. A face, action or quiet view can open without a teaser. Respect required chronology; titles and departures need not come first.

Develop the structure through events, changes of scale or emotional stakes. Connect the strongest action, reaction, reveal or relationship to its setup. Let energy rise and settle, aligning major turns with musical phrases when appropriate. While selecting moments, use [editorial_captions.md](editorial_captions.md) for optional observations and callbacks suited to the brief. Keep the visual evidence they depend on and leave room without commentary.

Compare ending ranges against the proposed last passage: a completed action, response, farewell or closing view. In a piece about people, a readable exchange may resolve more than an unrelated scenic or object photo. A place or object can close when it completes the central idea. Honor a required ending, allow reading time and reassess closure after rearranging the cut.

## Cut and Transition Deliberately

Cut on action, gaze, movement, phrase or information change. Preserve direction, eyelines and screen position, or clarify the change with a wider view. For a featured action, identify its visible result and reaction before placing the cut. With successive turns, finish the last turn retained or omit it as a unit; a complete earlier turn does not resolve a later one. A truthful adjoining view can carry the outcome. Protect speech and musical meaning.

Use dissolves for softness or time passing when the overlap stays readable. An outcome must register before a fade, overlay or incoming image obscures it, not merely occur before the source clip ends. Prefer a clean cut or neutral bridge to distracting double faces or ghosted motion. A real crossfade needs overlap; non-overlapping fades dip through black.

For source-audio fades or J/L cuts, lower or mute the video's audio and place that source on an audio track with aligned source/output times and the required fades or offsets. Follow [timeline.md](timeline.md) and mark musical fit or continuity unverified when listening is unavailable.

For a `mute_audio` source (stray speech or unwanted source music), lay a **same-scene ambient bed** beneath the muted range. Extract a clean, dialogue-free and music-free range from material already available for that scene, put it on an audio track with measured source/output times, and listen across the cut. Do not substitute another scene's sound or BGM. When no suitable bed exists, retain silence and record it in the delivery notes. This is editorial guidance and an assembly reminder; it does not authorize a new generation call. Revalidate and review the changed audio.

## Compose a Coherent Frame

Choose the canvas from the brief and source orientations. When a reference video is provided, follow its canvas and layout unless the user explicitly asks for a change; evaluate readability within that design rather than replacing it with a different layout. Before repeating a layout, compare the weakest important composition at viewing size, including photo cards and the ending. A clearer scene cannot stand in for this check.

When the important face, gesture or action result is hard to read:

- Compare the proposed layout with the same source fitted plainly to the canvas, at equal display size, when the brief permits layout changes. For an unconstrained photo card, also try reducing secondary text and decoration. Preserve required reference layout, text and decoration rather than silently redesigning them.
- Preview a promising crop through the action's beginning, development and result. Identify which face, gesture or object becomes readable and inspect movement near the edges. Preserve the people, hands, objects and context needed to understand the action.
- If layout cannot solve it, inspect a clearer source interval or alternate view; omit an optional weak shot if it adds nothing. Keep a wide view when geography or a group relationship is its purpose. Enlarging low-resolution footage cannot recover detail; disclose unavoidable limits for required material.

Implement the clearest treatment preserving the action and required reference style. Use consistent margins, type and colors; enlarged silhouettes in blurred fill can compete with the subject. Match distracting exposure or color jumps while preserving natural skin and intentional lighting. For repeated white-outs or severe flicker, compare a clearer interval or view, preserving meaningful sound. A flash's source origin or short duration does not make it a good opening; dimming clipped whites cannot restore detail. Shorten, move or omit obstructive captions only when they are optional and the brief permits it; disclose conflicts instead of silently changing required captions or reference layout.

Render crop, reframe, exposure or stabilization changes into derived assets and observe them before use. Verify readability again in the final composition with captions and overlays. The timeline fits video inside the canvas; a layout intention in metadata does not change pixels.

## Review as an Audience Member

After rendering and technical QC, use `video_observe` at viewing size, covering all sections and important cuts. Follow featured actions through the last retained result and reaction, including during transitions. A few frames beside a cut cannot establish completion. Listen where sound matters when playback is available; stills cannot verify timing in motion or audio quality.

Check the brief, photos and captions, and revisit these vulnerable parts where the task permits the proposed alternatives. Preserve required content, reference layout and pacing even when a different edit would be shorter:

| Part | Compare and decide |
| --- | --- |
| Opening through the first meaningful moment | Is the attraction clear? If setup delays it, shorten or replace the lead-in using the candidates, within the user's order constraints. |
| Repeated activity | Reassess all activities at their final cumulative durations, including restored footage and later returns. Does each added passage develop what is already there? Revisit the main remaining repetition concern or rushed outcome. |
| Least readable composition | Compare the prepared layout, reframe or source alternatives at the same viewing size. Verify that the chosen treatment makes the subject legible throughout the action. |
| Ending | Compare the final passage with the ending candidates. Does it complete the main subject or feeling, with time to register the result? Honor explicit ending requirements. |

Apply justified changes, render and QC again, then inspect affected passages and adjoining cuts. Preserve defining actions, reactions and useful caption setups and callbacks as the whole cut changes. Follow the caption reference when their pictures change. Record decisions, checked ranges and compared previews in the existing notes, including what a rejected shorter candidate lost; "builds atmosphere" is not verification.

Finish when issues are addressed or meaningful limits disclosed. Distinguish sampled frames, playback and listening in the review summary; runtime and clean QC alone cannot establish a better edit.
