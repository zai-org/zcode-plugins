# 3D Short Film

Your task is to make a 3D short film from the given script and reference images.

Design a complete director's script, storyboard, and detailed action and scene choreography; then generate the art assets according to the whole-film plan, and complete model actions, scenes and final-film polish item by item.

## Workflow:
0. Read the whole script and all reference images, then plan the whole film: settle the style; director's script, storyboard and scene design; art-asset plan, on-screen subject actions and blocking plan; dialogue plan.
0.1 Style: state the art style of the whole film, for example cartoon / pixel art / realistic / ...
0.2 Scene breakdown: how many independent scene segments the film has; each scene's set, the environment props it needs and the spatial layout it needs; what the foreground, midground and background each need, and how they are arranged.
0.3 Director's script: for each scene, the plot design, character actions, blocking, interactions and dialogue
0.4 Storyboard design: the camera angles when shooting, camera placement and moves, camera motion paths, and the layout of characters and shot sizes in the recorded frame
### Art asset generation
Once the style is settled, generate art assets in one consistent style:
1. Simple models, environments, prop assets and the like: implement them yourself in code. Make sure each generated model is complete and correct from every viewing angle, the style is consistent, and the props are varied.
1.1 For self-made assets, attend to each object's material, texture and physical properties. Rigid bodies, non-rigid bodies, liquids and gases must be clearly distinguished, using different modifiers or materials to simulate how well they conform; liquids and gases need their transparency and flow shape considered.
1.2 The environment needs the necessary, plausible set-dressing props that fit the art style
1.3 Self-made models need simple textures that you build yourself; an untextured white model must not be used as a final asset.
2. Complex models that code cannot produce (subjects with fine textures): call model generation. The steps are:
2.1 First check whether a final character three-view turnaround that meets the requirements has been provided (T-pose or A-pose, no serious occlusion or merging of parts, and a clean white-background image of at least 256x256 obtainable for each view). If not, call `image_generate` to generate a T-pose four-view sheet combined in a single image: white background, every part clearly separated with no occlusion or merging, and the joint articulation points clearly visible.
2.1.1 For a character that has reference material images, the reference images relevant to the final design sheet must be sent to the image generation tool; with several images, check them for duplicates first, then combine them into one collage. The prompt must not describe what is in the material images. It may only: specify the four views (front, left, back, right), the pose, no occlusion/merging, the white background, the layout order in the image (1 * 4 or 2 * 2, depending on the character's proportions), any additions, removals or changes relative to the material images (not required), how the sub-images relate when a collage is used (for example the character on the left and the target outfit on the right), and a special style the user specified (if any);
2.1.2 For a character with no reference material image, describe the required character design clearly in the prompt.
2.1.3 By default the generated image follows the given order; do not change it on your own.
2.2 After getting the four-view sheet, crop it yourself into 4 view images and, following the interface description of `model3d_generate` (`operation:"generate"`), pass the four views in the correct order (front, left, back, right) to get a textured GLB model of the whole subject.
2.2.0 The tool returns `preview.png`, the baseline render of the model's appearance. The render setup must first reproduce the baseline's materials, textures and shape; changing how the model renders is forbidden. After importing the model, render a front view with your render setup, put it side by side with `preview.png`, and check that it faithfully matches. Once it passes, the render style may afterwards be changed only through lighting, the world and post-processing effects; modifiers and added material slots are likewise forbidden.
2.2.1 Models exported in different batches may face different ways (some face −X, some face +X). After import, determine the facing automatically from the position of the eye bones relative to the head bone, and normalize it.
2.2.2 The style of the detailed 3D model itself is treated as fixed once the final design sheet step (2.1) ends. Any modification of the 3D asset's data objects (including the render setup) for the purpose of adjusting or changing the style is forbidden. The appearance of the final rendered model must stay consistent with the preview.png returned by `model3d_generate`. If you judge that it does not meet the user's style requirement, treat this task's style instruction as unachievable: give up remedial actions and finish the film accepting the defect.
2.3 After getting the GLB model, call `model3d_generate` (`operation:"rig"`) to rig it;
2.4 In all later operations, do not adjust the model asset on your own unless you find a serious problem (for example mesh components that were not separated into layers, causing severe tearing in motion).
2.4.1 The model asset includes all object data: meshes, vertex groups, armature, material slots, node trees and texture images.
2.4.2 Edit boundary: only small, local repairs are allowed, for example a very few interpenetrating vertices, a texture flaw covering a small share of a single component, or a local fine-tune of a small share of the weights. Changing the overall position, orientation and scale is allowed. The "serious problem" exception in 2.4 above may also be handled only by local repair.
2.4.3 Forbidden for any reason:
- Replacing the original weights wholesale;
- Cumulative vertex modifications exceeding 10% of the whole model's vertex count; when this happens you must stop modifying, review all the modifications and produce a new, compliant modification plan, see 2.4.4.
- Rebuilding the mesh or the armature;
- Global modifications: including but not limited to whole-model retopology, re-unwrapping UVs, recomputing whole-body weights, and global changes to materials / specular / roughness and the like;
- Replacing a whole structure, such as swapping the head, a limb, or a prop/part.
2.4.4 When the model or rig has a defect that small local adjustments cannot fix, you must send four-direction renders of the pose that exposes the defect, plus a recording of the defect (optional), to `c2v-3d-reviewer` for an independent model-check review, to prove it is a real, serious defect. Once it is proven, you may look at the images the model was generated from to determine whether the four-view sheet or the model generation is at fault, and judge whether regenerating is certain to solve it. Anything not clearly solvable is recorded as an unsolvable defect, and you finish the film accepting it. For a clearly solvable problem, at most one rebuild is accepted. The style, shading and texture look covered by 2.2.2 are not rebuildable defects of this kind.
### Actions, scenes, and final-film polish and rendering
3. With the rigged model, start implementing the main actions in the director's script. For each action, carefully refine how plausibly the rigged model's joints move, carefully tune each part's motion path and speed, and make full use of the model's skeleton, so the action is smooth, natural and consistent with dynamics. Reversed joints, clipping, and stiff or unnatural motion are forbidden. Use still high-resolution frames to judge whether a pose is plausible, and consecutive time-ordered frames to judge whether the motion is plausible.
3.0 First use one or more representative pilot shots to develop and validate the shared library: the common layer every shot depends on, such as character import / IK / prop attachment / soft contact / rendering. Write the remaining shots only after the pilot validation runs through (still-frame pose self-check / consecutive-frame motion self-check / clipping report all meet the bar). After changing the shared library, self-check the shots it affects.
3.1 When a choreographed action turns out to be impossible for the model (arms too short, head too large and so on), revise the action/shot design in the script sensibly to fit the actual model; for example, if the hand cannot reach the top of the head directly, lower the head first and then touch it. If it still cannot be done, do not force it: change it to an achievable action close to what the script asks for. Distinguish between impossible and a current bug; cutting back script completeness over a fixable problem is forbidden.
4. After each key action is done, start recording scene by scene. Focus on refining how the subject interacts with props and the environment: across the whole timeline of an interaction, whether the positions of subject and prop obey physics, whether there is clipping, whether the interaction and blocking are plausible and smooth, and whether the camera work is sensible.
5. After actions and scenes are done, the self-check must include both high-resolution keyframes (for looking at static poses) and consecutive time-ordered frames (for looking at continuous motion). Every pose held for more than 1 second and the first and last frame of every shot must be self-checked at full resolution; self-checking static poses from low-resolution contact sheets alone is forbidden.
6. Polish actions and scenes serially, one action/scene at a time; developing all actions/scenes in parallel in one go is forbidden.
7. Render the whole film and add suitable transition animations.

### Clipping self-check

1. BVH overlap
During action implementation (3.) and scene recording (4.) of the actions, scenes and final-film polish and rendering stage, every update must be self-checked for surface-intersection overlap between the subject and the props with `mathutils.bvhtree.BVHTree`, to determine whether there is clipping.

- Which frames to test: the first and last frame of every shot, poses held for more than 1 second, and the before, during and after frames of every contact event (biting, pouring, picking up, lying down, pressing, opening a door).
- Which pairs to test: the character mesh against every other mesh object in the scene, enumerated by the script. The check script must include all objects; removing or altering the checked objects is forbidden, and so is any whitelist/exemption. Add two non-overlap checks: whether the camera position is inside any mesh, and whether the head's bounding box leaves the frame.
- How to get the geometry: you must take the armature-deformed mesh with evaluated_get(dg) after evaluated_depsgraph_get(), and multiply by matrix_world.
- Output and pass criteria: write the clipping report to out/qc/geocheck_<scene>.json, each row giving the frame number, the object, the number of intersecting face pairs and the center of the intersection region;
- Closing the loop: the pass criterion is all zeros. Every non-zero entry must be inspected and fixed; if it cannot be fixed, you must produce a high-resolution freeze-frame image of the clipping object and decide from the image whether it is truly unfixable / a reasonable overlap caused by the action. After changes, re-run until it is back to zero, and record in the latest clipping report where the final high-resolution freeze-frame image of each non-zero entry is; only then may you proceed to the review dispatch in 3.2. The clipping report must be sent to the review together with the rest.

2. Ray parity test: cover the blind spot of BVH overlap, which is full containment without intersection, by adding a ray parity test of "whether several of the character's bone points are inside a prop's volume".


## Result review sub-agent
The `c2v-3d-reviewer` sub-agent is provided for independent visual verification
1. Reviews are of two kinds: defect review of generated assets, and defect review of the animation work;
2. For the defect review of generated assets, see 2.4.3 above
3. For the defect review of the animation work: provide only the recording paths with their corresponding scripts and the location of the clipping report; specifying a verification scope or hinting at your own subjective intent is forbidden. Put the recordings submitted for review under `out/`.
3.1 The sub-agent returns feedback on visual problems: problems with pose, motion, scene, interaction and so on. Fix the visual problems it reports, giving priority to the serious visual problems in the review result that would affect delivery.
3.2 After the main action choreography (3.) and the scene recording (4.) are complete and your self-check finds no problems, you must call the sub-agent for an independent visual check; one call may provide several videos with their corresponding scripts and clipping reports.
3.3 Improve the work according to the review feedback; work module by module, and do not repeatedly re-render scenes/actions unrelated to the current change.
3.4 Each workflow stage may call the independent review no more than 2 times (that is, at most 4 times in total overall).
3.5 For a problem that 2 independent reviews have not resolved, wrap up quickly; spending more budget on a problem unresolved after 2 reviews is forbidden. When the 1st review of a workflow stage contains visual problems that affect delivery, you must send the 2nd review after fixing them and completing your self-check.
4. During a review, limit the CPU usage of render processes and leave CPU headroom for the review process.

## Usage notes

Each tool's own description is authoritative for its parameters and rules; this section only adds what the tool descriptions do not cover.

**Delivery**: the final film must be rendered and written to `out/final.mp4` (playable, with a positive duration); this is the delivery criterion for this task.

**Four-view sheet (2.1) with `image_generate`**
- One four-view sheet is one entry in `items`; use `kind:"character"` for the subject. The four views are combined in one image, so set `check_panel_layout:false`; otherwise it is mistaken for a storyboard grid and flagged falsely.
- This task needs a white background: state a pure white background in the prompt.
- `size` is a resolution tier, one of `1K` / `1.5K` / `2K` (default `2K`): use `orientation:"landscape"` for a 1×4 layout and `orientation:"square"` for 2×2; at `2K` every view is at least 256×256.
- For a subject with reference images, pass the reference image paths in that entry's `reference_images`.
- Write the output to `output_dir:"out/code2video/assets"`; transient failures are retried automatically.
- Rule of thumb: **the left view is the side seen by a camera on the character's left, where the character's face points toward the left of the image; the right view is the side seen by a camera on the character's right, where the character's face points toward the right of the image.** "Left/right" in the four-view sheet follow this definition, not the character's own left and right hands.
- The image-generation prompt must state both the camera position and the face direction for each side view, the two consistent with the rule of thumb, for example "LEFT SIDE view: camera on the character's left, face toward the LEFT edge of the image".
- During tuning you may directly render a SMALL number of still frames at specified frames, and delete them once viewed. Previews that need consecutive frames or finished passages, and the final render, are all output directly as mp4 per shot (FFMPEG container, H.264); extract single frames for self-checks from the video by timestamp with ffmpeg and delete them once viewed. Keeping PNG frame sequences is forbidden.

**Generation and rigging (2.2, 2.3) with `model3d_generate`**
- Before submitting, look at which way the face points in the two side views: the one with the face toward the left of the image goes in `left`, the one with the face toward the right goes in `right`. Fill `views` by the actual face direction, not by the panel labels. A label that disagrees with the direction means the image-generation prompt was written wrong; just swap them by direction when submitting, with no need to regenerate the image. Adjusting the order by reasoning without having looked at the images is forbidden.
- Precondition for regenerating the same character (generating again under a different `output_name`): first render the existing model from four directions (one each of front, left, back and right, written under `out/`), and dispatch the four renders together with the original four-view sheet to `c2v-3d-reviewer`. Regenerating is allowed only when the reviewer confirms a specific defect (a face on the back, no back side, left and right features swapped and so on), and the review conclusion must be stated in the delivery notes. Redoing without a review conclusion is forbidden; redoing under a new name is a new generation task.
- Do not cancel a job while it is collecting its result; if the download fails, call again with the same arguments to collect it.
- When the tool returns `not_configured`, handle it the same way as `account_limit` or `usage_limit`: stop calling it, make the model assets yourself, and state this truthfully in the delivery notes.

**Blender**
- The container has Blender 4.5 (the `blender` command). Drive it with scripts: `blender -b --python <script>.py -- <args>`.
- Blender's bundled Python is used only inside Blender; the container's `python3` cannot `import bpy`.
- Render with Cycles and choose the `CUDA` device (OptiX is unavailable); this works on any kind of GPU. EEVEE is usable only on consumer GPUs (such as a 4090); on an H100 (check with `nvidia-smi -L`) one EEVEE frame takes ten-plus minutes or even longer, because that kind of card has almost no graphics-pipeline units, so do not use it.
- At startup the render script must explicitly set `compute_device_type='CUDA'`, enable the devices, set `scene.cycles.device='GPU'`, and print the device list. After the first frame finishes, check in the log the device `Cycles` reports and the `nvidia-smi` utilization; if the GPU is at 0, stop immediately and investigate.
- Run render jobs as `run_in_background` background tasks with completion notification, and avoid polling;
- Before submitting a render job you must first work out the GPU utilization, to keep render jobs from blocking.

**Camera placement**
- Avoid close-up shots of the 3D model; keep some distance
- Adjust the camera angle so the composition is sensible and pleasing

**Technical advice**
- Prefer Blender IK constraints over FK; when the IK constraints show an action is unreachable, revise the action design in the script.
- For soft materials (such as cloth props) that need to conform to the subject, take the fit into account.
- For render polish during development you may build a decimated model (forbidden for the final-film render or for delivery review), usable only for tuning actions/interactions. When building the decimated model, its rendered appearance also takes `preview.png` as the baseline and must not differ too much from the final-film look (otherwise the action/interaction tuning results cannot be trusted). This step exists only to lower the render cost of tuning; using it as a reference for, or to optimize, any style or final-film render look is forbidden, and for that reason it is not subject to the asset-modification restrictions in 2.2.0 and 2.4.

**Tool calls**
- Change code with Edit, in the same turn as the render Bash call that follows it; chain the geometry self-check and the contact-sheet generation into the render command, so one command produces one image; the next turn can go straight to Read.
- Polling with sleep is forbidden; use Bash's run_in_background parameter, and you are notified automatically when the task ends. Run long renders in the background with that parameter and write the next shot's code while waiting for the notification; nohup & plus sleep is forbidden.
- While the previous shot renders in the background, write the next shot; still-frame tests can render several shots together, and several contact sheets can be read in one turn.
